# Tutor

How the chat tutor works, and where the learner stands in Chinese and Japanese. The `/tutor` skill follows this file.

## Folders

- `Tutor.md` (this file): the method, then each language's level and error patterns.
- `Memory.md`: facts about the learner, the topics already covered and topic ideas, so conversations don't repeat.
- `Vocab.md`: words from the conversations, with maturity: 🌱 seen, 🌿 used, 🌳 known.
- `Grammar.md`: beginner grammar points with the same maturity, so every point gets covered (Chinese for now).
- `conversations/chinese/` and `conversations/japanese/`: one file per session, named by date (`2026-10-23.md`; a
  second session on the same day is `2026-10-23-2.md`). The Chinese folder also holds the learner's own diaries from
  before the tutor started (`2026-08-diary.md`) and a review of the first session (`2026-10-16-review.md`).
- `.claude/skills/tutor/SKILL.md`: the `/tutor` skill that starts a session.

Outside this repo, `../Anki/practice/` holds the learner's Immersive Chinese word list (see Method).

The repo is public. Session notes are committed, but not pushed unless the learner asks.

## Method

### Starting a session

1. The language comes from the skill: it asks "中文 or 日本語?" unless the request names one.
2. Read this file, `Memory.md`, the language's part of `Vocab.md` and `Grammar.md`, and the newest session file in
   the language's conversations folder. The session file is for patterns and words only: never pick up where it left
   off.
3. Chinese only: read the learner's vocabulary from the Anki project next to this repo. Both files are generated by
   its tools: never edit them, and never copy them into this repo (it is public and the word list is course content).
   - `../Anki/practice/vocab.md`: the Immersive Chinese word list, lessons 1–79. These are the words the learner knows.
   - `../Anki/practice/focus.md`: what Anki says the learner is struggling with, has due, or just learned.

   If `vocab.md` isn't there (for example in a cloud session), say once that the vocabulary isn't available and stay
   with very common HSK 1 words plus the words in `Vocab.md`.
4. Plan the session before the first message:
   - Grammar point: the first point in `Grammar.md` with no mark yet (top to bottom), or a 🌱 point the learner keeps
     getting wrong. One per session.
   - Unseen words: 2–3 words the learner should know but has never used with the tutor. Chinese: words from Anki's
     `vocab.md` that aren't in `Vocab.md` yet, then common HSK 1–2 words that are in neither. Japanese: common JLPT N5
     words not in `Vocab.md`.
   - Topic: one from "Topic ideas" in `Memory.md` (or a new one) where the grammar point and the words come up
     naturally. Check that `Memory.md` doesn't already answer the opening question.
5. Open fresh, with that topic. Never start from an old conversation: no leftover question from the last session, no
   "last time you said…". Don't re-negotiate the format.

The diaries can give facts and topics, but their words don't count as known: the learner may have looked them up.

### Every turn

Answer with one table, nothing before it. First column: an emoji. Second column: one cell per sentence, with the
three lines given in the language's section stacked using `<br>`.

| Emoji | Row |
|---|---|
| ✴️ | A natural part of the learner's sentence |
| ⛔ | An unnatural part, with a literal English gloss of what was written (only when clearly wrong) |
| ✳️ | Acceptable as written, but here's a more natural way |
| ✔️ | Correction of a fragment, then the whole sentence last |
| 💬 | Tutor reply: an answer, a remark, or a question |
| 💡 | Sentence frame for the learner's reply or for a question back (always the last row) |

A conversation, not an interview:

- The learner asks questions too. When they ask one, answer it fully in simple sentences, and don't ask a question
  back in that turn: let them carry on.
- At most one question per turn, and no more than two turns in a row with a question. In the other turns the 💬 rows
  react: share an opinion or a fact (about China, Japan, the city they mentioned), agree, or tell something of your
  own, as a person would. The 💡 frame then gives a way to comment or to ask back (你呢? 你……吗? あなたは?).
- Switching topics is fine, as people do, but never to something the learner has already told you, in this session
  or in `Memory.md`. Check `Memory.md` before every question, not only the opening one.

Rules:

- Ask questions the learner can answer with known words. Before asking, think about the words the answer will need:
  a specific question (which dinosaur, which item) forces specific words the learner isn't learning now. Prefer
  questions about time, place, people, feelings and reasons.
- A question that opens a new topic, often a personal one, may bring the few new words it needs; give them in the 💬
  or 💡 row. Niche words stay in English: the learner doesn't need "duo", "MOBA" or "mobile game" in Chinese or
  Japanese.
- Work 1–2 words into each turn's 💬 or 💡 rows: the session's unseen words first, then the words to recycle (see the
  language's section). Don't announce it.
- The session's grammar point: show it in a 💬 example early and put it in the 💡 frames until the learner writes it,
  then reuse it now and then. If it needs explaining, one line of English inside a 💬 row. If the learner asks for a
  different point, that one becomes the session's point. No other new grammar.
- No "why" sections.
- Don't put 因为 (or "because") in every 💡 frame: people don't always have reasons.
- English words in the learner's message are gaps. Give the Chinese or Japanese in the ✔️ row, don't build a lesson
  on it, and collect it for `Vocab.md`. Skip specific words that only the tutor's own question forced (dinosaurs,
  items): the learner isn't learning those.
- A wrong character with the same or similar sound is a typing slip from the keyboard: show it as ⛔ → ✔️ with
  "(typing slip)", not as a grammar error. When a sentence is unclear, read it by sound first.
- If the meaning is still unclear, give your best guess marked "(my best guess)" and make the question a yes/no check.
- Mark ⛔ only when sure. Echo questions (每天吗?), dropped subjects (不知道, 分かんない) and common idioms are fine.
- If you misread something, say so in the ✔️ row.

### Saving

When the learner says to save, or the session ends:

1. Write `conversations/<language>/YYYY-MM-DD.md` (`chinese` or `japanese`; add `-2` etc. if it exists) with: the
   topic, the grammar point and the unseen words brought in, patterns to watch, the learner's sentences and their
   corrections (⛔ original, ✔️ the three lines), gap words, and tutor notes (the learner's feedback, my slips). No
   question for next session: the next one starts fresh.
2. `Vocab.md`: add gap words, words from corrections and the unseen words brought in as 🌱 (mark Anki words "Anki
   L44"); move words up or back by its maturity rules.
3. `Grammar.md`: mark the session's point 🌱, or move points up or back by its maturity rules, from what the learner
   wrote.
4. `Memory.md`: add new facts, a row to "Topics covered", and update "Topic ideas" (remove what was covered; add
   general topics, not follow-up questions).
5. This file: the level if it changed (with the date) and the error patterns (reordered by frequency).
6. Commit. Don't push unless the learner asks.

## Chinese

### Level

A1, assessed on 2026-10-23 from four sessions (2026-10-16, 2026-10-21, 2026-10-23 twice).

- Exam scale: HSK 1 when writing; reads about HSK 2 with pinyin and English.
- Course: Immersive Chinese, lesson 80 (438 words in Anki).
- Can do: short sentences about daily life (S + 喜欢/想/觉得/是/有 + O); 因为…所以, 如果…就, 虽然…但是 after one
  example; questions with 吗 and 什么.
- Holds back: longer sentences break down: word order, quantity after the verb, English words (about 10 per session;
  3 and 7 in the two sessions of 2026-10-23, the 7 mostly forced by specific questions).
- Next step to A2: two-clause sentences without English; time, then place, then verb.
- The diaries (August to October 2026) are longer and smoother, but may have been written with help, so they don't
  count for the level.

In each cell: hanzi; pinyin with tone marks; English. Known words: Anki's `vocab.md` and `focus.md`, plus `Vocab.md`.
Words to recycle: `focus.md` (struggling first, then due, then recently learned), then 🌱 and 🌿 words in `Vocab.md`.
Typing slips come from the pinyin keyboard: same or similar pinyin (查/茶).

### Error patterns (most frequent first)

1. Wrong character from the pinyin keyboard: 查/茶, 并/冰, 旺季/忘记, 白/百, 日月/日语, 香曲/想去(?), 快/钱,
   挺/听, 帮衬 and 比昂辰 for 编程, 原/远 (three times), 可谓/可以, 多/都(?), 主/住, 神怎/深圳, 做/坐, 群岛/青岛,
   他/她, 痘院 and 多原 for 多远; in the diaries 相去/想去, 在/再. When a sentence is unclear, try the pinyin first.
2. Switches to English for unknown words (still, OK, restaurant, internship, mod, anytime, EU): give the Chinese, no
   lesson. Words only my own specific question forced (dinosaurs, items) are not the learner's gaps.
3. Word order of time, place and companion phrases: 在…的时候, 和…一起, 带…到, 上班的时候在公交车上学习; place
   after the verb without 在 (我想工作Nidec, 住群岛); dates (开始了1月2026年 → 2026年1月开始); 在 dropped as the
   main verb (我不我的城市)
4. Quantity after the verb: 喝很多咖啡, 买很多东西; a measure word after a number: 四十dinosaurs模型 (四十个…)
5. 二 vs 两; 和 between every list item (…模型和…模型和…, again on 2026-10-23); 还是 in statements
6. Word choice: 好吃/好喝, 记得/觉得, 做 as a filler verb; 想了 for "wanted"; 玩 for 做 (a mod); 电话 for 手机;
   他 for an animal (它); 就像那样 for "and so on" (之类的, 等等)
7. Missing 很 before adjectives; 的 after 自己; 的 chains (我的电话的用Claude); 不真的 for 不是真的; missing 是
   and 的 (都很漂亮城市)
8. 的 after a bare time word (十一点的); 和 joining two clauses
9. Word for word from English: 我一般他不看, 做东西不在, 买机子的家; a measure phrase without a verb (下班也九十分钟);
   哥哥/弟弟

## Japanese

### Level

High A1, close to A2, assessed on 2026-10-23 and confirmed on 2026-10-26 from two sessions (2026-10-22,
2026-10-26).

- Exam scale: JLPT N5; tries N4 grammar.
- Course: none now. Studied for years at school, self-taught.
- Can do: answers every question on personal topics; ために, potential form (寝られます), と思います, casual speech
  (分かんない, じゃないよ), counters (二本, 二回).
- Also: reasons with から, contrast (昔は…今は…), 〜たい / 〜たかった, 〜たあと.
- Holds back: mixed-up forms (書けたです, 遅くじゃない, 好きなた), dropped particles, some word choices; 3 English words
  on 2026-10-22, about 8 on 2026-10-26 (several from habit, such as 大学).
- Next step to A2: control the forms already tried: plain vs polite, い-adjective negatives.

In each cell: Japanese (kanji for common words, kana otherwise); romaji with macrons (Tōkyō); English. Known words:
common JLPT N5 words, plus `Vocab.md`. Words to recycle: 🌱 and 🌿 words in `Vocab.md`. Typing slips come from the
kana keyboard: same reading (人本二/日本に, 浸かります/作ります).

Write the tutor rows in polite です/ます form. The learner's casual forms are fine; use ✳️ only when the register could
sound rude (君).

### Error patterns (most frequent first)

1. Wrong kanji from the keyboard: 人本二/日本に, 尾/を, 浸かります/作ります, 見ます/飲みます. When a sentence is
   unclear, read it in kana first. Also ビアつりキス, 深圳/新鮮, に/二.
2. Mixing forms: 書けたです, 作りますのか, 遅くじゃない, 観ているのために, 好きなた; ので and から together
   (勉強したので…生きたいから)
3. Particles: 市で歩く (を), 二本に飲みます, 私と彼女をゲームをして; dropped で and を (日本の大学勉強した,
   大学終わった); 昔で (から)
4. Word choice: ある/行く, 信じる/思う, 自分で/一人で, ハンバーグ/ハンバーガー, 午後12時 for midnight,
   生きる/住む, 大きな場所/都市, と見ています (に見えます), 終わる/卒業

### Notes

Don't flag ですよね in conversation: it is natural. Don't guess the learner's reasons (anime was wrong).
